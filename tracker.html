    How the status logic works

    The real workbook has 4 linked tabs:

    • Dashboard: totals by document type: registered, received, at the desk, collected, and delay levels.
    • Case Tracking: one row per case, filled from the Formatter. Formula columns look up the other tabs and work out each document's status and the overall case status. Contact emails are used to tell applicants when documents arrive.
    • Delivery Arrival: documents arrive from headquarters in a sealed delivery every two weeks. Staff scan each document's barcode as it's opened and record the arrival date.
    • Counter Handover: applicants sign a paper slip at the counter. At the end of the day, staff scan all signed slips in one go.

    Each document is Collected, At the desk, or, if it hasn't arrived, rated by how long it has been open (see the colour legend above). The overall case status is, in order of priority: Archived · Chase · Ready to collect · Partial (some documents here, some on the way) · Alert · On track.

    01Problem

    The old spreadsheet only logged documents when they arrived. There was no view of earlier or later stages, so staff kept answering status enquiries by hand and delays were noticed late.

    02What I built

    Each case tracks its ID card and passport separately, and an overall case status shows what needs attention, so the team only acts on the exceptions (management by exception). When a delivery arrives, staff scan each document's barcode, and the workbook finds the case and updates its status on its own.

    Key decision

    Cases are flagged at 40 and 60 days → so only delayed ones need attention.

    03Result

    One scan per documentReactive → proactive

    Each arriving document is one barcode scan: the tracker finds the case and updates its status automatically. With the Formatter, processing a delivery went from ~4 hours to under 30 minutes, and the team now tracks cases proactively instead of reacting to status enquiries.
