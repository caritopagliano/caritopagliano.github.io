01Problem

The old spreadsheet only logged documents when they arrived. There was no view of earlier or later stages, so staff kept answering status enquiries by hand and delays were noticed late.

02What I built

An Excel workbook with 4 linked tabs, run with a USB barcode scanner. Each case tracks its ID card and passport separately, and an overall case status shows what needs attention, so the team only acts on the exceptions (management by exception).

  • Dashboard: totals by document type: registered, received, at the desk, collected, and delay levels.
  • Case Tracking: one row per case, filled from the Formatter. Formula columns look up the other tabs and work out each document's status and the overall case status. Contact emails are used to tell applicants when documents arrive.
  • Pouch Arrival: documents come from headquarters in a sealed diplomatic bag (the "pouch"). Staff scan each document's barcode as the bag is opened and record the arrival date.
  • Counter Handover: applicants sign a paper slip at the counter. At the end of the day, staff scan all signed slips in one go.

Each document is Collected, At the desk, or, if it hasn't arrived, rated by days open: On track (up to 40 days), Alert (41–60) or Chase (over 60, raise with headquarters). The overall case status is, in order of priority: Archived · Chase · Ready to collect · Partial (some documents here, some on the way) · Alert · On track.

Key decision

Cases are flagged at 40 and 60 days → so only delayed ones need attention.
